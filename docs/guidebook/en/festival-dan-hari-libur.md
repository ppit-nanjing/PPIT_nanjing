## Why you need to know this from the start

During major holiday periods, travel gets busy, campus can be quiet because many students go home, and many services follow holiday schedules. Sources recommend identifying major festivals early so you can plan travel times and daily matters. The two things most often affected: transportation tickets and service opening hours.

## Seven major festivals

- **Spring Festival / Chinese New Year (Imlek)** — January or February. The biggest holiday period, with the busiest homecoming travel rush.
- **Lantern Festival** — after the Spring Festival. A celebration of culture and family atmosphere.
- **Qingming Festival** — spring. A short national holiday in many places.
- **Labor Day** — early May. One of the busiest travel periods.
- **Dragon Boat Festival** — early summer. A traditional holiday, and zongzi are commonly found.
- **Mid-Autumn Festival** — September or October. Mooncakes, family gatherings, and cultural events.
- **National Day / Golden Week** — early October. Heavy travel; transport and tourism are busy.

Sources state the timing in rough terms as above, that is, the month or season, not an exact date.

## Impact on transportation

- Spring Festival becomes the largest homecoming travel period. National Day brings heavy travel with busy transportation and tourism, and Labor Day becomes the next busiest travel period.
- If you do travel during a national holiday or a major student travel period, book tickets earlier or avoid it if possible. Spring Festival, the late-summer arrival period, and National Day can be very crowded.
- High-speed rail is cited by sources as the most practical way to move between cities, including for weekend trips, visa matters, and travel during holidays.

## Impact on services and campus

- Campus can feel empty because many students return to their home regions.
- Many services follow the holiday schedule, so opening hours may differ from regular days. Check first before going out, especially for service offices and your regular eateries.
- Goods and daily necessities are best prepared before the long holiday, not once services have entered the holiday schedule.

## Intercity travel during holidays

- Short trips to nearby places make more sense in the first semester than ambitious long-distance plans.
- Long-distance travel only becomes easy once your phone, payments, and campus routine are stable. Before that, tickets and accommodation are more of a hassle.
- Learn how to get around your own city first before drawing up big travel plans.

## Before a long holiday

- Check the holiday schedule for the services you usually use, because their operating hours may differ from regular days.
- If you have to travel during a peak period, book tickets early; if it's not urgent, it's better to avoid that period.
- Make sure your phone, payments, and campus matters are all settled before planning a long trip.
