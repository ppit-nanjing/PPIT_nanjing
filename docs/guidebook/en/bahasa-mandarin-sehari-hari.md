## How to Use

You don't need perfect Mandarin for daily matters. A few short sentences are enough to buy food, ask for directions, and talk to staff. Speak slowly and use short sentences.

If needed, show your phone: a written address, a package pickup message, or a route on a map. At stations, hospitals, dorm gates, and package pickup points, information you can look at together is often understood faster.

## On Campus

The international student office (国际学生办公室, guó jì xué sheng bàn gōng shì) is the first stop when you need to deal with campus administrative matters.

- 国际学生办公室在哪里 (guó jì xué sheng bàn gōng shì zài nǎ lǐ) — where is the international student office.
- 我需要去国际学生办公室 (wǒ xū yào qù guó jì xué sheng bàn gōng shì) — I need to go to the international student office.
- 我是学生 (wǒ shì xué sheng) — I am a student.

## If You Don't Understand

If the conversation starts to get complicated, settle one thing first. Say 我不太明白 (wǒ bú tài míng bai), then 请说慢一点 (qǐng shuō màn yì diǎn).

If it's still not clear, ask the person you're talking to to write it on paper or on their phone, or show the written address and the map on the screen. Typing one short sentence in a translation app is often faster than repeating the conversation.

## Notes

The pinyin in this article has no tone marks, so read it only as an approximation of the sound.

Listening practice with the people around you is more useful than memorizing the entire table.
