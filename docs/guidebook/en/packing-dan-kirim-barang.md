## Packing Principles

Pack for the first two weeks, not for your entire course of study. Bulky items, things that are easy to buy locally, or room-specific items are better bought after you arrive. Check your baggage allowance first before you start packing so there are no extra fees at the airport.

The checklist below is a practical reference, not a rule. The goal isn't to bring your entire life with you, but to arrive with the supplies for the first stage. One small tip: put a sticker on your suitcase so it is easy to recognize.

## Checklist by Category

- **Documents** — passport, visa documents, admission letter, proof of insurance, accommodation details, flight booking confirmation, emergency contacts.
- **Money** — bank cards, enough RMB cash or an emergency fund, and one backup payment method. Around 1.000 RMB in cash is usually enough for initial needs.
- **Electronics** — phone, laptop, charger, plug adapter, power bank, earphones.
- **Clothing** — everyday clothes, one light jacket, one warmer layer if needed, comfortable shoes, sleepwear, underwear. Check the destination city's weather; most places are in full summer and new clothes can be bought after settling in.
- **Health** — personal medication carried in the cabin, glasses or contact lens supplies if needed.
- **First night** — toothbrush, toothpaste, small toiletries, tissues, one set of spare clothes.
- **Comfort items** — small towel, refillable water bottle, snacks for the journey, one familiar personal item.
- **Academic tools** — a few pens, a notebook, transcripts or certificates if needed, and one small folder for campus documents.
- **Seasonal items** — an umbrella is needed for summer.
- **Worship or personal comfort** — small items that support worship, routines, diet, or emotional comfort during the first month.
- **Transit travel** — neck pillow, sandals (useful on the plane), an empty water bottle for refilling, luggage tag, and one printed address card.

## Things Better Bought After Arrival

- **Bedding** — dorm mattress sizes vary.
- **Laundry supplies** — easy to buy locally.
- **Hangers and storage boxes** — easier once you've seen the room.
- **Rice cooker or kettle** — only buy if dorm rules allow it.
- **Bulky winter items** — depends on the city and the actual climate.
- **Large-size toiletry refills** — heavy to carry and easy to buy locally.
- **Desk lamp** — room lighting quality varies, check the desk first.
- **Fan or heater** — depends on dorm rules, the season, and what's already provided.
- **Kitchen tools or cookware** — many dorms restrict heating appliances or don't support cooking.

## Cabin Baggage Rules

On departure day, keep your passport, boarding pass, phone, charger, and destination address together in one bag that is easy to reach. Arriving at the airport too early is rarely a problem; being late can turn a simple trip into a hassle. Allow enough time for baggage drop, security screening, immigration, and walking between gates.

Before security, check the basics: liquids are restricted in cabin baggage, sharp objects should not be placed where they trigger screening issues, and most airlines require power banks and spare lithium batteries to stay with you, not in checked baggage. If an officer asks, answer briefly and honestly: you are a student, you are heading to the university, and your admission documents are ready.

## Door-to-Door Shipping

Door-to-door suitcase services help lighten the load. One of the largest providers reaches 175+ countries, including China. There is also a guide to shipping luggage for international students to decide whether sending items separately is worth it.

The goal is not to move all your belongings in one trip. If it's just a short language course or one semester, one suitcase is usually enough. But if you're doing a full degree with winter jackets, books, or sports equipment, or if you land in one city and then take a train to another, shipping some items separately can make things much easier. Arriving with one manageable suitcase is far calmer than moving several suitcases at once on the first day.

For comparing flights, student fares, and accommodation for the first night, set up one travel booking platform before you leave. Student discounts, extra baggage allowance, and foreigner-friendly hotel filters can make a real difference. International student booking platforms offer discounts on hotels, plane tickets, high-speed trains, and even temporary SIM cards.

## Declaration and Customs

If you are shipping personal items separately, read the customs instructions carefully. On arrival, some items may need to be declared as unaccompanied baggage, and stamped forms must be kept for customs clearance. Keep every receipt, item list, and stamped form — a small missing document can cause big delays after arrival. If this step is skipped, the shipment can become slower, more expensive, or more complicated to release.

Check the customs requirements of the departure country and of China before you start packing.

## Items That Cannot Be Shipped

- **Passport and original documents** — keep them with you in your carry-on baggage.
- **Prescription medication** — carry it yourself, with supporting documentation in two languages if necessary.
- **Lithium batteries and power banks** — carry them yourself; do not ship them.
- **Food and snacks** — avoid shipping them in boxes; they can be bought in China as well.

## Lost or Delayed Baggage

If the suitcase does not appear on the belt, report it before leaving the airport arrival area. Prepare your baggage tag, boarding pass, and a photo of the suitcase. Ask for a case reference number, contact method, and the expected delivery process.

Because of this, one set of spare clothes, medication, a charger, and important documents should be kept in carry-on baggage, not in checked baggage.
