## Dormitory or Rental

Campus dormitories are usually the easiest starting point for the first year: cheaper, simpler to arrange, and close to student life. The trade-off: less privacy, more rules, and uneven room quality.

If you can't get a dormitory, renting a private apartment gives you more space and freedom, but more costs, deposits, contracts, and setup. Renting a house as a foreigner isn't easy; sources suggest large platforms such as Wellcee and Lianjia, or asking the school for help. If you arrive earlier than scheduled, confirm with the school first, because the dormitory sometimes isn't available yet. See the room before buying anything.

## How to Get a Dormitory Place

At most Chinese universities, international students live in dormitory buildings separate from local students. Domestic students are usually in rooms of four to six people, while international dormitories more often have single or double rooms; the cost is generally higher.

Roommates cannot be chosen in the first year; rooms are allocated randomly or based on study program. If you have a strong preference or a medical reason, contact the accommodation office early, but be prepared to accept the standard allocation.

A dormitory room is not automatically guaranteed. In Beijing and Shanghai capacity is limited, and some universities state they cannot guarantee campus accommodation for all international students. If the school opens a room booking window, watch the schedule and secure your place quickly; missing the deadline means renting off campus with a larger deposit and agency fees.

## Check the Room on the First Day

Check the room with a checklist: mattress size, number of outlets, whether the shower runs well, toilet fixtures, how the door locks, where parcels are placed, and how laundry and drinking water work in your building. Also check the campus network, windows, light, and noise.

Many dormitories are more practical than comfortable: bunk beds, shared facilities, shared laundry, building access rules, and separate water or electricity systems. Access systems can be strict, from entry cards to rules about coming home at night; at some schools, coming back very late gets your name recorded.

If you share a room, communicate about lights, sleep hours, guests, food smells, and alarms, and keep your part clean.

## Electricity, Water, and Laundry

A campus dormitory is not a hotel. At some schools, electricity is topped up through an app, hot water is billed separately, laundry is a shared room, and room internet requires a campus package or student login. Drinking water may come from a dispenser, a filter machine, or delivered bottles; dryers can be limited.

Start with items for your first week: bedding, one pillow, power accessories, toiletries, and laundry supplies. Plug adapters (转接头) are easier to buy online; make sure they fit your plug. Other items are available at local supermarkets or via delivery:

- 排插 (power strip)
- 床上用品（三件套 or 四件套）(bedding set)
- 枕头 (pillow)
- 洗衣袋 (laundry bag)

## Food and Parcel Delivery in the Building

Student canteens are usually the easiest and cheapest place for routine meals, especially in the first month; large campuses may have several canteens. Eating in the canteen is cheap and quick, restaurants off campus are more expensive, and delivery can cost more than the canteen. Large universities often have halal food windows or halal canteens; ask the school early.

For parcels, some dormitories only allow delivery to the gate or lobby, some use parcel racks, and some buildings do not allow couriers upstairs. Almost all couriers cannot speak English, so prepare a text chat or a translation app.
