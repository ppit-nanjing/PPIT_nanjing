## Working and Interning While Studying

A student visa in China prohibits work. There are no exceptions for part-time work, informal work, or even short-term work — all of it requires prior approval, even if it is only temporary.

Internships are allowed, as long as they are officially registered. The order is:

1. Notify the campus international office before starting the internship.
2. Make sure the internship is officially registered through the campus.
3. Apply for an internship annotation on your residence permit at the Exit and Entry Administration (出入境) — the same office that issued your residence permit.

That approval must come before you start working or interning, not be reported after the fact. Paid work without the correct documents is considered a serious violation: your visa can be revoked and you can be deported. If you are unsure about a job or internship offer, ask the campus to review it first before accepting, and do not start until the matter is clear.

## SIM and Driving

In this section, SIM means driver's license, not a phone SIM card. International driver's licenses are not recognized in China. If you still want to drive, the option you can arrange is a temporary driver's license at the 车管所 (local vehicle administration office).

- **Validity period** — up to 3 months, or up to 1 year if your period of stay is longer.
- **Requirements** — passport, residence permit, and a valid driver's license from your home country that has been translated into Mandarin.

Most students find that public transport, ride-hailing, and bicycles are enough for daily life and that they don't need to drive. Details on city transport are in the inner-city transport article.

## Prohibitions You Often Aren't Aware Of

- **Drugs** — 100% illegal with no exceptions, including if they're legal in your home country. This is about drugs, not prescription medicine. The penalties are severe, so don't take the risk at all.
- **Sensitive material on school or public networks** — don't access NSFW, private, or other sensitive material over the campus network or public wi-fi.
- **Taking photos** — don't photograph anything related to the military, government buildings not open to the public, or official vehicles on duty.
- **Shipments abroad** — if you buy or send goods for someone abroad in a volume exceeding reasonable personal use, declare them and pay the tax.
- **Anything suspicious** — if something feels off, ask the people around you for help.

## Frequently Asked Questions

- **Can I work part-time without documents?** No. A student visa prohibits paid work, including informal work or just a few days.
- **I already did an internship through the campus — do I still need annotation?** Yes. Internship annotations are applied for at the Exit and Entry Administration (出入境).
- **Is my international driver's license valid?** Not recognized. Get a temporary driver's license at 车管所 if you really need to drive.
- **Can I photograph military buildings from a distance?** Do not photograph anything related to the military, non-public government buildings, or official vehicles.

## When in Doubt, Ask the Campus

For matters of work, internships, and residence permit annotation, the safe route is the campus international office. Its staff are the ones who check the completeness of documents and guide you on which documents need to be handled at 出入境. Immigration rules can change, and this guide may fall behind the latest on-the-ground situation.
