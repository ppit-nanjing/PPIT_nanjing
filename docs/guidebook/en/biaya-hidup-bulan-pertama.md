## One-time expenses at the start

The first month is usually more expensive than expected because of one-time expenses: bedding, transportation, rental deposit, paperwork, small room supplies, and other miscellaneous purchases.

For those living off campus, the deposit is the biggest expense. The source's cost table states that off-campus rent for foreigners usually requires a 6-month deposit upfront. Elsewhere, the source states that the Chinese rental market generally uses a 2- to 6-month deposit, plus an agency fee of about 1-2 months' rent if going through an agent. For first-night supplies, the source suggests buying or borrowing first.

- University application fee, usually 400 to 600 RMB, non-refundable and not covered by scholarships.
- Seat confirmation deposit at some universities, after you receive the offer.
- Tuition for self-funded students is 20,000 to 40,000 RMB per year.
- If you get a scholarship, some campuses still require the first-year tuition to be paid in full first; the reimbursement goes into a Chinese bank account in the first semester after enrollment.

## Monthly costs

The living cost figures in the source are written in RMB (元). Nanjing falls into Tier 2 with an estimate of 2,000 to 3,500 RMB per month excluding tuition. Tier 1 is 2,500 to 5,000 RMB, Tier 3 is 1,800 to 3,000 RMB.

- Campus dormitory: 250 to 1,200 RMB.
- Off-campus rent: 1,500 to 4,000 RMB.
- Food and groceries: 500 to 1,500 RMB. Generally 600 to 1,000 RMB; if you cook yourself it can be 400 to 600 RMB.
- Metro/bus transportation: 100 to 300 RMB. A single metro ride is 3-8 RMB, HelloRide is 1 to 2 RMB per trip. Those who regularly commute back and forth usually spend 100 to 200 RMB.
- Utilities (water, electricity, internet, mobile data): 50 to 350 RMB. Air conditioning in summer can push electricity up to 250 RMB.
- Daily necessities shopping: 100 to 1,000 RMB, generally 150 to 300 RMB.
- Entertainment: 50 to 500 RMB, the majority below 200 RMB.

Costs that are often forgotten: internet service 50 to 100 RMB per month, printing documents 20 to 50 RMB, medicine and minor room repairs 25 to 50 RMB, course materials 100 to 200 RMB in the first semester, cigarettes 200 to 300 RMB.

For off-campus rent, the source's Tier 2 table: a room in a shared apartment 1,500 to 2,800 RMB, a studio or your own 1-room 3,000 to 5,000 RMB, utilities 150 to 400 RMB, home internet 40 to 100 RMB.

## Estimated first-month total

The source does not give a single figure. Students in standard dormitories who eat in the cafeteria and live simply are said to be able to get by on 1,400 to 1,800 RMB per month in a city like Shenzhen, without dormitory fees and on a tight budget. A more comfortable but still reasonable figure is around 2,000 to 2,500 RMB per month. The first month is roughly the size of your city's monthly figure plus the one-time costs at the start; the source does not break down the combined total.

## How to save

- Divide expenses into three groups: mandatory needs in the first week, useful upgrades in the first month, and optional comforts that can be postponed. Those who hold back usually save more.
- Leave a small buffer for transportation and unexpected costs.
- Eat in the cafeteria, about 20 to 50 percent cheaper than eating out; one full portion is 8 to 15 RMB. Some target 20 to 30 RMB per day.
- If the budget is tight, rent together with classmates.
- For scholarships, ask the admissions office when results are announced and whether recipients must pay in full first and then be reimbursed. Keep all receipts.
- Cigarettes at 200 to 300 RMB per month are cited by some respondents as the biggest expense. The source's advice: do not smoke or drink alcohol if you want to save.

## Notes

The figures above are written by the source in RMB (元). The source does not give an exchange rate to rupiah, so do not assume these figures are in rupiah. All figures are estimates and may differ by city, room type, and lifestyle. Confirm tuition and rental deposit with the campus or the contract owner before paying.
