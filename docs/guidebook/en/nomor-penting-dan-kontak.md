## Emergency Numbers

Save these numbers in your phone before you leave, not after you arrive. The four numbers below work throughout China and are used for situations that can't wait.

| Service | Number | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Police | **110** | Crime, danger, or need police immediately |
| Fire department | **119** | Fire or smoke |
| Ambulance | **120** | Medical emergency; in the city guidebook called First Aid |
| Traffic accident | **122** | Accident on the road |

Additional useful numbers, all from the city guidebook:

- **114 — Telephone number inquiry.** To ask for the number of an office, shop, or institution. The staff speak Mandarin, so have the place name ready in Chinese characters.
- **12345 — Municipal government service hotline (mayor's hotline).** For public services, complaints, and administrative questions in Nanjing.
- **12110 — Police alarm SMS.** A text channel to the police, used if making a call isn't possible.
- **025 8442 0114 or 8442 0005 — Public Security Bureau Nanjing**, at No. 1 Honggongci, Sanyuangang. This is the city-level police office, not an emergency number.

These numbers generally serve English. When calling, state the location first in a short sentence; in an emergency the location is the first and most useful piece of information. If there's immediate danger, call the number first, then contact the campus.

The local police station (派出所, pàichūsuǒ) is used for administrative matters: residence registration within 24 hours if you stay outside a registered dormitory, loss reports, and certificates.

## Campus Contacts

Some campuses list their numbers in their own guidebooks, others do not. The ones that do not are what you need to gather yourself before departure:

- **International student office** — visa matters, residence permit, registration, and official letters.
- **Advisor or coordinator** — one person you can contact when a problem comes up outside office hours.
- **Dormitory office** — if you live in a dorm, for room issues, building access, or lost items.
- **One trusted friend** — so there is someone who can come or contact your family in Indonesia.

Ask for these numbers during orientation or through the admission email, then save them under a clear name, not just a number with no description.

Its history, the nature of the organization, and its official channels are in the article **PPIT Nanjing Branch Profile**.

## How to Store

- **Save in Chinese characters.** Place names and addresses in hanzi are easier to read for taxi drivers or officials. Copy them exactly from official sources, don't retype from memory.
- **Make three copies.** On the phone, one paper note in the wallet, and one backup in the cloud. If the battery runs out or the signal is lost, the paper note is still readable.
- **Screenshot important numbers and addresses.** Also save photos of the passport page and visa so they can be shown without opening the app.
- **Send them to family in Indonesia.** Give them the China emergency number, the campus and dormitory address, and the international office contact, so they can help from afar.

## Notes

- Emergency numbers apply nationwide, but call rates, office service hours, and representative office phone numbers may change. Recheck via official channels if this has not been updated for a long time.
- The list of hospitals, student insurance, and emergency medical handling is in the article **Health and Emergencies**. This page is only a list of numbers, not a guide to seeking medical care.
- For matters at the China level, do not use the Nanjing branch contacts; use the central PPIT channels listed in the profile article.
