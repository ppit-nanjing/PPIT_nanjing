## Emergency Numbers

Save these numbers on your phone as soon as you arrive in Nanjing. In an emergency, state your location first in a short sentence.

- **110 — Police.** For crime, danger, or to ask for police help immediately.
- **119 — Fire Department.** For a fire or if there is smoke.
- **120 — Ambulance.** For medical emergencies; at the source it is called First Aid.
- **122 — Traffic Accident.** For accidents on the road.

If there is immediate danger, call the number first, then contact the campus. These numbers generally provide service in English.

## If You Get Sick

- **Carry your passport.** The passport is the identity document used when seeking medical treatment. Also keep a photocopy of it and your family's emergency contacts.
- **Show the address in Chinese characters.** Save your dorm address and the hospital address in Mandarin writing on your phone, then show it to the driver or the registration clerk. Stating the location correctly helps more than explaining at length.
- **Look for the international patient counter.** Large hospitals usually have a special desk for foreign patients, so ask about that first before queuing at the general counter.
- **State the location first when calling.** For a medical emergency, call 120, state the location, then contact the campus international office and the dorm office.

For the mandatory medical check-up for new students, registration is done through the official WeChat account 江苏国际旅行卫生保健中心, menu 业务预约. Arrive according to the schedule and bring your passport, 2 copies of 3×4 photos with a white background (photos taken within the last 6 months), 600 RMB examination fee, and the results of a medical check-up from the last 6 months if you have them. Results can be collected in physical form (sent to the address you filled in, 3–5 days) or online via the WeChat account (a few hours).

## Minor Complaints

Fever, cough, a cold, headache, or diarrhoea are usually handled at the campus clinic or the nearest pharmacy first, not at a large hospital.

- **Campus clinic** — ask the international office for its location and opening hours during orientation. For minor complaints the queue here is the shortest.
- **Pharmacy (药店 yaodian)** — almost every block has one, many open 24 hours. Describe the complaint in short sentences or show it through a phone translator.
- **Common medicine** — paracetamol for fever and pain, oral rehydration salts for diarrhoea. Keep the medicine you normally use from home if that is what you are used to.
- **If it does not improve within two days, or the fever is high** — go straight to a hospital in the list above and bring your passport.

## Insurance

Health insurance is mandatory for all international students studying in China. The process is easiest to handle online before registration, and the payment proof should be kept together with your other documents.

- **How to buy in Nanjing** — search for and follow the 来华保服务 account on WeChat, enter your personal details, then purchase. This account handles insurance consultations, policy checks, and claims for foreigners coming to China.
- **Cost** — 800 RMB per year. This package covers medical treatment and accidents.
- **Payment** — only via WeChat or Alipay. International bank cards such as Visa and Mastercard cannot be used.
- **Note** — insurance schemes may differ depending on the campus. After purchasing, save the screenshot or the payment proof.

## Infectious Disease Prevention

The city guidebook includes a 2024 epidemic prevention guide. It is simple, but useful for your first season.

- **Masks and vaccination** — during outbreaks of respiratory diseases such as influenza and COVID-19, avoid crowded places and wear a mask properly. Wear a mask at medical facilities, schools, or elderly care and social welfare institutions, and when close to people with respiratory symptoms. If you have a fever, sore throat, or cough, wear a mask and seek care promptly. Influenza vaccination is recommended.
- **Hand washing and ventilation** — respiratory pathogens spread through droplets and surfaces. Ventilate rooms 1–2 times a day for 0.5–1 hour each time. Air out and wash bedding and clothing regularly, and take out the rubbish daily instead of letting it pile up in the room.
- **Do not swim in the Yangtze River** — May to October is the high-risk season for schistosomiasis (known as big belly disease). Avoid swimming or playing in the Yangtze River and the waterways connected to it, especially where there are warning signs. If your skin comes into contact with water containing schistosomes, symptoms such as fever, abdominal pain, and diarrhoea can appear within a month. Go to a community hospital in the riverside area for screening promptly and tell the doctor about your water contact. More information is available from the local disease control institutions.
