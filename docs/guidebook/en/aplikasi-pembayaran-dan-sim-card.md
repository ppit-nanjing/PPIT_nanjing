## The Most Practical Order

Start with a reliable internet connection. Once online, sort out payments, maps, ride-hailing, and food delivery. A local +86 number follows for app verification, school notifications, courier calls, and daily communication. If your phone supports eSIM, that can be a bridge for the first few days.

Many campuses have a telecom outlet on or near campus during orientation week. Registration uses a passport, sometimes plus an admission notice or student card.

Daily life in China runs on RMB (人民币 Renminbi), also called CNY or yuan. Daily payments are almost entirely digital, but still carry a little cash as backup. Payments don't have to be sorted out on day one: one working payment method is enough. A local bank account is still necessary, so bring your passport and student documents.

## Apps Used Daily

Don't install all the apps at once. A practical order: messaging, payment, shopping, maps, then ride-hailing; entertainment comes later.

For chat and payment there's 微信 Wechat and 支付宝 Alipay. Wechat and QQ sometimes ask for extra QR code verification when registering with a foreign number or recovering an account, so students usually ask a friend who's already in China for help.

Maps and transport: 地铁通 Metroman which supports English, 高德地图 AMap, and 弟弟出行 Didi Taxi for late-night arrivals or when carrying a lot.

Shopping: 淘宝 Taobao, 拼多多 Pinduoduo for cheap options from trusted sellers, 闲鱼 Xianyu for second-hand goods, and 1688. Food delivery uses 美团 Meituan (外卖).

Travel: 中国铁路 for high-speed trains, 去哪儿 Qunar, 携程 C-trip, and 同城 Tongcheng. Entertainment: NetEase Cloud Music, QQ Music, iQIYI, and bilibili, which is also useful for learning the language. RedNote has lots of information on daily life.

Some apps have separate domestic and international versions, for example Douyin and TikTok, Feishu and Lark, and Tencent Meeting and VOOV.

## SIM Cards and Internet

Nanjing has three internet providers: China Mobile 中国移动, China Unicom 中国联通, and China Telecom 中国电信. To get a SIM card from any of the three, you must have a Chinese address and register with a passport.

Prices and connectivity differ among the three, and sometimes there are special student prices. An example from China Mobile: 60 yuan per month for 80 GB internet and wifi around campus. The range is 50 yuan to 100 yuan per month, depending on the promo. Monthly payment can be through the app, the WeChat Mini App, or Alipay.

## Ordering Food and Receiving Packages

For your first order, save one reliable address format, one campus gate or pickup point, and one simple sentence to send if the courier calls. First check whether couriers are allowed into campus, and write a fixed drop-off point in the notes field.

Avoid peak hours and don't stray far from your phone until the order arrives. If the courier calls, just mention the gate number, building name, or campus pickup shelf, or send a short message and share your location from the map app.

For packages, check three things: the pickup location, the pickup code 取件码, and the pickup deadline. Showing the message alone is usually enough even if your Mandarin is limited. Many places use self-service machines: enter the code, take the item, then close the door.

If the package can't be found, don't panic: show the message, code, and your name or phone number. If the message is only in Mandarin, take a screenshot and use a translation app. If the location is still unclear, ask the dorm guard or the package room staff rather than guessing with the wrong code.

## Notes

- Couriers almost certainly can't speak English. Keep a translation app and your campus address in Mandarin.
- Make sure your local number is active for delivery calls.
- Small cash amounts are still useful before payment apps are ready.
